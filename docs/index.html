EA-TDC

Treasury contribution to deposits: estimates and transmission.

This work asks whether the long-history Tier 2 estimate of Treasury Deposit Contribution produces an early deposit response, how that result changes after selected credit/rate lags, and what remains under narrower source-side comparisons imported from `tdcpass`.

Claim hierarchy

What the release claims.

The public package is intentionally narrow: long-history Tier 2 deposit pass-through is the headline, the same-treatment residual is a perimeter/plumbing-sensitive offset, broad mortgage/core-loan crowding-out is weak after selected lags, and consumer credit remains a guarded candidate margin.

Headline

Tier 2 deposit response.

The selected-lag long-history surface delivers the main public result.

Boundary

Residual is not a channel.

The same-treatment non-TDC deposit component is treated as perimeter and plumbing-sensitive.

Credit

Broad crowding-out weakens.

Mortgage and strict core-loan rows are weak after selected lags; consumer credit is guarded.

Sensitivity

Questions

Research questions.

The central questions are whether Treasury Deposit Contribution produces an early deposit response, whether that result survives selected credit/rate controls, and how to delimit residual and credit evidence without over-naming mechanisms.

TDC definition

Equations and notation.

View equations

The baseline treatment is the marketable-Treasury, transaction-based quarterly approximation to Treasury Deposit Contribution. In the upstream estimator its short name includes "bank" because it excludes credit unions, not because it excludes the rest of world. The implemented baseline formula below still includes Federal Reserve, bank-sector, and rest-of-world Treasury transactions.

Headline estimator

Baseline TDC estimate used in the quarterly results

This is the implemented quarterly approximation used in the main EA-TDC results. It includes Federal Reserve, bank-sector, and rest-of-world Treasury transactions, subtracts Treasury operating cash, and adds positive Federal Reserve remittances.

\[\widehat{\Delta D}^{mkt,bank}_{TDC,t} = \left(\Delta TS^{tx}_{Fed,t} + \Delta TS^{tx}_{Banks,t} + \Delta TS^{tx}_{ROW,t}\right) - \Delta Cash^{tx}_{Treasury,t} + Remit^{+}_{Fed,t}\]
ΔD^mkt,bank_TDC,t
Estimated quarterly approximation to Treasury Deposit Contribution used in period t.
ΔTS^tx_Fed,t
Federal Reserve net transactions in marketable Treasury securities.
ΔTS^tx_Banks,t
Bank-sector net transactions in marketable Treasury securities.
ΔTS^tx_ROW,t
Rest-of-world net transactions in marketable Treasury securities.
ΔCash^tx_Treasury,t
Change in Treasury operating cash transactions; higher Treasury cash drains deposits before they reach domestic nonbank deposits.
Remit^+_Fed,t
Positive Federal Reserve remittances to Treasury, summed within the period.
Theory identity 1

DU-facing definition

The theory object is Treasury Deposit Contribution to domestic nonbank deposits: net Treasury payments to DUs, plus Treasury debt service to DUs, plus net Treasury-security sales from DUs to RUs.

\[\Delta D^{TDC}_{DU} = \left(G^{ND}_{DU} - R^T_{DU}\right) + DS^T_{DU} + \left(Q^T_{DU\to RU} - Q^T_{RU\to DU}\right)\]
ΔD^TDC_DU
Treasury Deposit Contribution to domestic nonbank deposits.
G^ND_DU
Non-debt-service Treasury spending paid into DU deposits.
R^T_DU
Taxes and other non-Treasury-security receipts paid from DU deposits.
DS^T_DU
Treasury-security debt service paid into DU deposits.
Q^T_DU\to RU
Cash settlement value of Treasury securities sold by DUs to RUs.
Q^T_RU\to DU
Cash settlement value of Treasury securities sold by RUs to DUs.
Theory identity 2

Treasury-cash constraint

The same theory object can be re-expressed through reserve-side Treasury-security settlement, RU-facing Treasury cash flows, actual positive Fed remittances, and Treasury operating cash.

\[\Delta D^{TDC}_{DU} = \left(Q^T_{DU\to RU} - Q^T_{RU\to DU}\right) + \left(I^T + R^T_{RU} + \Pi^F_T - G^{ND}_{RU} - DS^T_{RU}\right) - \Delta TOC\]
I^T
Treasury-security issuance proceeds received by Treasury at cash settlement value.
R^T_RU
Taxes and other non-Treasury-security receipts paid by RU sectors.
\Pi^F_T
Actual Federal Reserve remittances transferred to Treasury during the period.
G^ND_RU
Non-debt-service Treasury spending to RU sectors.
DS^T_RU
Treasury-security debt service paid to RU sectors.
ΔTOC
Change in Treasury operating cash, including the TGA, TT&L, and related operating balances.
Theory identity 3

Residual deposit decomposition

This is a diagnostic cross-check rather than the headline estimator: start from deposit change, subtract the major non-Treasury deposit drivers, and treat the remainder as TDC.

\[\Delta D^{TDC}_{DU} = (\Delta M - \Delta C - \Delta X) - (\Delta L^B_{DU} + \Delta A^{B,NT}_{DU}) - \Delta A^{CB,NT}_{DU} - \Delta F^{NT}_{DU} - \varepsilon\]
ΔM
Change in the chosen money aggregate that includes DU deposits.
ΔC
Change in currency held outside DU deposits.
ΔX
Other non-deposit money components included in the selected aggregate.
ΔL^B_DU
Net bank lending to DUs.
ΔA^B,NT_DU
Domestic-bank non-Treasury asset flows to DUs.
ΔA^CB,NT_DU
Central-bank non-Treasury asset flows to DUs.
ΔF^NT_DU
Foreign non-Treasury flows to DUs.
\varepsilon
Timing, reclassification, and other residual measurement error.

TDC means Treasury Deposit Contribution: the contribution of Treasury-related cash flows and Treasury-security transactions to changes in domestic nonbank deposits. The quarterly headline on this site is an implemented approximation to that theory object, not a full direct DU ledger.
